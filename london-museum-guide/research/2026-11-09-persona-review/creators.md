# Creators, facts and visitor questions for the two museum guides

Researched 9 November 2026. Audience figures are from each YouTube About page or video that day unless another source is named.

## The short version

- **All 8 Watch links in the Natural History Museum (NHM) guide are live** (YouTube oEmbed). Three have better replacements. **The Science Museum guide has none**, though its `app.js` supports them; eight are proposed below.
- The guides already carry nearly every famous fact I found. **What they lack is credit to the people who told those stories, and a myth-busting card.** The Science Museum guide names no curator.
- Safest to point to: the museums' own scientists and curators, then PBS Eons, Numberphile, *In Our Time* and Darren Naish.

## 4. What visitors ask

Comments were read on 9 November 2026.

- **Practical.** The Science Museum's [visit FAQs](https://www.sciencemuseum.org.uk/visit/visit-faqs): tickets on the door? Can I leave and re-enter? What if I miss my slot? Gallery pages add: is it free, where, when?
- **Dinosaurs.** Paul Barrett's [Q&A](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/your-dinosaur-questions-answered.html): has every species been found, where are fossils found, what killed them, which was biggest. Comments on [How do dinosaur fossils form?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=87E8bQrX4Wg) (5.2m views): how did they get buried, and where in the UK can I find fossils?
- **"Is it real?", "Where did Dippy go?"** These come up under the [Hope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NekXGq25lrk) videos (Dippy is a plaster cast). "Two brains?" is fact 5.
- **Cheddar Man** ([comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TQ8dc0XhUMM)): "Was he African?" "How much Neanderthal DNA?" "When did Europeans turn pale?"
- **Science Museum.** On the [Burnley engine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1g0bNpKf1jw) video: "When is it running?" No times are published, so the guide should say "ask staff". [Apollo 10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CeeHXyNqeUI) draws claims that the landings were faked. On [Numberphile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G2_Q9FoD-oQ): "How long would a modern computer take to break Enigma?" "Where is Stephenson's Rocket?" comes up too: it has been in York since 2019, as `stops.js` notes.

## 5. How the guides could use this

- **"Go deeper", at most three per stop:** a museum video, a podcast and an independent creator. Each shows the name, the length and "Opens YouTube" or "Opens BBC Sounds". Fill the existing `video.minutes` and `video.views` fields.
- **Credit lines**, for example "Checked by palaeontologist Darren Naish against the museum's 1981 history". The Science Museum stories could name Russell, Millard, McEnroe, Rooney and Jackson.
- **"Myth or fact?" card:** facts 2, 5, 7, 8 and 9, plus the "Darwin monkeys" (folklore, no primary source) and the faked-landing claim. Each card gives the verdict, one line of evidence and a source.
- **Ask the Guide starters** from the real questions above, such as "Is it real?" or "When does the engine run?", rather than invented ones.
- **Licensing:**
  - Link out only: never download, re-host or clip a video. If you embed one, use YouTube's own player.
  - Facts may be restated in our own words; wording and images may not be copied.
  - Science Museum Group images are CC BY-NC-SA, labelled per object. NHM data-portal images are CC BY by default.
  - Links need a signal, so mark "Go deeper" as online only.
  - iPlayer is UK only; BBC podcasts play anywhere; Audible needs an account.
